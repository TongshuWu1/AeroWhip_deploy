## 3. Units, frames, and state meanings

Sources: [state.py](../simulation/state.py), [configuration.py](../simulation/configuration.py), [coupled.py](../simulation/dynamics/coupled.py).

- SI units: metres, seconds, kilograms, newtons, and radians. Gravity points along negative world Z.
- Commanded and measured drone position refer to the **top-plate tracking origin**, centered in XY, not the center of mass (COM).
- `R` maps body coordinates to world coordinates. Scene quaternions use XYZW order. Angular velocity is expressed in the body frame.
- The internal rigid-body state is COM position, body-to-world rotation, COM velocity, and body angular velocity.
- Free cable-node positions/velocities are world-frame quantities. Node 0 is the drone attachment, reconstructed from rigid-body motion rather than independently integrated.

With body-frame offsets `c` (tracking origin to COM) and `a` (tracking origin to attachment):

```text
p_COM = p_tracking + R c
p_attachment = p_tracking + R a
v_attachment = v_tracking + R (omega_body x a)
```

Distinguish three trajectories throughout analysis and the paper:

1. **Requested command:** desired P/V/A/yaw sent to the modeled controller.
2. **Predicted motion:** physical state produced by the coupled model responding to that command.
3. **Measured motion:** recorded OptiTrack/flight data, with its own verified timing and quality.

A command is not the model's predicted actual trajectory. Actual drone acceleration in rollout results is a velocity difference, not the commanded acceleration feedforward.

## 5. Coupled dynamics and effective controller

Sources: [coupled.py](../simulation/dynamics/coupled.py), [dder_math.py](../simulation/dynamics/dder_math.py), [coupled_control.py](../simulation/dynamics/coupled_control.py).

### Mechanical model

- Six-DOF rigid drone with translational and rotational dynamics, including yaw and angular velocity.
- Isotropic cable bending, curvature-rate damping, configured external drag, gravity, and edge-length constraints.
- A free-pivot attachment: root position follows the drone kinematically, with no separately transmitted attachment bending moment. Reaction forces can still generate drone torque through the nonzero attachment arm.
- Root-node inertia contributes to the coupled mass system once. Forces, damping, and length-constraint impulses affect drone translation, body rotation, and free cable nodes in the coupled solve; the root is not a prescribed external trajectory.
- A substep assembles the coupled system, solves damping and velocity constraints, advances position/rotation, applies iterative position corrections, and projects velocities again.

The attachment displacement map has a small three-by-six body block and an identity block for free cable nodes. The implementation multiplies only that body block when mapping constraint/curvature Jacobians, directly retains the free-node columns, and constructs root inertia and isotropic drag from their body/free-node blocks. Position/velocity mass projections build only the three-by-six attachment and six-by-six body blocks they consume, omitting unused full-matrix padding while preserving the body arithmetic. It preserves attachment translation/rotation coupling and free-node masses. These are shared algebraic execution changes, not approximations to the physical model. The dense bending-damping product and implicit solvers remain unchanged; the existing compiled-only tridiagonal mass-projection path is also unchanged. See the retained optimization checks in Section 9.

Finite-step constraint projection is an approximation. Numerical regularization is not a physical model of sharp folds/contact. No cable self-collision, ground contact, aerodynamic rotor model, or motor allocation model is implemented in this dynamics path.

**Known damping limitation (2026-09-22):** the current regularized curvature-rate operator is not exactly objective under rigid rotation of a curved cable: a controlled rigid-motion test produces nonzero internal bending damping. Translation invariance, static coordinate-rotation covariance and positive-semidefinite dissipation do not establish this stronger property. CPU/CUDA counterexamples, an analytic expression, seven replay-state checks and four short unforced energy checks are retained in the [damping audit](../runs/physics_compact/20260922/damping_audit/summary.json). The unforced tests lost mechanical energy; the finding is not an observed energy explosion. The preceding [aggressive-motion study](../runs/physics_compact/20260922/aggressive_analysis/summary.json) found strong initialization sensitivity and unresolved full-horizon timestep convergence in the original and experimental optimized solvers. Shape-dependent damping contributes to local amplification, but the objectivity defect has not been shown to explain all long-horizon divergence. A standalone literature-based spin-aware kinematic reference passes controlled rigid-motion checks; it is not an implemented replacement dynamics model. Removing regularization, adding spin dynamics, changing fitted damping or promoting an optimized backend remains unapproved and unimplemented.

### Effective controller

For the already-delayed command, tracking feedback forms:

```text
a_eff = Kp * (p_d - p_tracking)
      + Kv * (v_d - v_tracking)
      + Ka * a_d
      + learned_drone_acceleration
```

Gains act componentwise. Hover trim supplies configured total drone-plus-cable weight. Desired attitude is built from the effective force direction and commanded yaw. Rotation error and angular-velocity damping produce body torque through the configured inertia. Collective thrust is projected onto the current body Z axis and clipped below at zero.

This is an **effective fitted response controller**, not an emulation of Crazyflie full-state firmware. There is no configured upper motor/thrust/torque saturation in this controller. Therefore command-limit validity is not proof of hardware feasibility.

The outer requested command is held constant over 0.01 s. The effective controller is recomputed from the evolving simulated state at each internal substep. The statement “100 Hz commands/feedback output” must not be confused with a claim that this internal response law is evaluated only once per outer interval.

## 6. Learned dynamics residuals

Sources: [residuals.py](../simulation/dynamics/residuals.py), [residual_fit.json](../models/residual_fit.json), and the selected checkpoint's embedded specification.

Two MLP branches use two hidden layers of 32 units with Tanh activations:

- Drone branch: 21 inputs -> 3 body-frame effective acceleration corrections, rotated to world coordinates and added to controller acceleration. Its features include command tracking errors, motion, gravity direction, and yaw-heading information.
- Cable branch: 20 inputs per free node -> 3 body-frame acceleration corrections. Features include local cable directions, relative position, velocities, material location, and a tip indicator. Corrections are rotated to world coordinates and multiplied by free-node masses before entering cable forces.

The selected checkpoint bounds each body-frame correction component with `0.5 * tanh(...)` m/s². This is a componentwise bound, not a 0.5 m/s² bound on the vector norm. The drone output is equilibrium-centered before its output nonlinearity. Cable corrections use a motion gate with configured speed scale 0.1 m/s and vanish at exact rest.

These are bounded **effective response corrections**, not identified material-force laws or a conservation-law proof. Equilibrium centering/motion gating do not establish global stability or generalization to unseen maneuvers.

## 7. Flight takes and fitting

Sources: [take_data.py](../take_processing/take_data.py), [library.py](../take_processing/library.py), [prepare_window.py](../fitting/prepare_window.py), [coupled_fit.py](../fitting/coupled_fit.py), [run_fit.py](../fitting/run_fit.py), [residual_windows.py](../fitting/residual_windows.py), [residual_fit.py](../fitting/residual_fit.py), [run_residual_fit.py](../fitting/run_residual_fit.py).

### Data preparation

1. Discover supported recordings in the selected folder and maintain a catalog of source paths/hashes. Importing a take does not authorize rewriting its raw files.
2. Inspect measured motion, explicitly linked desired/reference motion, recorded commands, gaps, and saved clock alignment in the take UI.
3. Prepare complete native-100-Hz measured windows; missing frames or invalid samples are rejected rather than silently filled/resampled.
4. Initialize pose from the final history sample. Estimate velocities from causal history, using the default 0.1 s history where requested. Reconstruct cable subdivisions from measured marker directions and configured interval lengths; enforce consistent initial length rates without moving the drone.
5. Look up the recorded command using source time plus saved clock offset minus model delay. Use left-endpoint zero-order hold. The command is already delayed in a `FitWindow`; the fitter must not apply another delay.
6. Compare each predicted next state to the measured state after the corresponding 10 ms command interval.

The fitting initializer's configured-length reconstruction is specific to measured data. The general model initializer validates a supplied cable state rather than silently projecting arbitrary inputs.

### Physical fit

The current parameter vector contains six bounded multipliers: position gain, velocity gain, attitude frequency, bending stiffness `EI`, curvature damping `Cb`, and external drag. Controller multipliers preserve the existing XYZ ratios. Geometry, measured/configured masses, inertia, feedforward gains, and command delay are not optimized by this fitter.

Optimization uses projected Adam in log multipliers. The loss combines tracking-position error scaled by 0.02 m, cable-marker position error scaled by 0.03 m, and rotation-matrix error with a 0.05 small-angle scale. These scales balance the objective; they are not success tolerances. Rotation error is a matrix-based surrogate, not an exact SO(3) geodesic distance.

Window weights balance maneuver families, then recording groups, then takes. The current split contains 20 training takes and 13 comparison takes across M3/M4 vertical-plane and M5/M6 horizontal-side groups. Recording groups are disjoint. They are historical data with prior exposure; do not describe them as a pristine unseen benchmark. M7 is not in this split.

### Residual fit and provenance

Residual fitting freezes physical parameters and optimizes only the two network branches. Current settings use 0.2 s segments, 0.1 s causal initialization history, Adam, gradient clipping, and a residual-magnitude penalty. Segment construction covers the full prepared windows, including an end-aligned tail segment when needed; equal segment counts preserve take weighting.

CUDA fitting batches windows/segments, uses graph capture, and can compile the physics substep. CPU physical fitting can parallelize independent windows. The fitting implementation supports gradients through its rollout machinery; this is distinct from the forward-only episode runner.

The retained local run `runs/residual_fit/20260921-000505/` contains `protocol.json`, `fit_result.json`, gradient-check records, before/after predictions, a hold comparison, and `summary.json`. Its records show 180 training segments, 50 updates, selected update 50, and termination at the update limit: **convergence was not established**. Initial/selected gradient checks are recorded as passing; this documentation update did not rerun them.

Provenance discrepancy to preserve explicitly: the archived summary still says `promoted: false` and `review_status: awaiting_user_review`, while the current default-model selector points to this checkpoint. The selector establishes what runs now; the historical summary is not a live model registry. Do not silently change either record to erase this distinction. The fit entry points do not automatically replace the default model.

## 8. Full-state commands and spline representation

Sources: [trajectory.py](../trajectories/full_state.py), [bspline.py](../trajectories/bspline.py), [bspline_torch.py](../trajectories/bspline_torch.py).

### Command contract

Full-state CSV column order:

```text
time_s,x,y,z,vx,vy,vz,ax,ay,az,yaw,omega_x,omega_y,omega_z
```

Time starts at zero with consecutive 0.01 s samples; no automatic resampling is performed. Body-angular-rate columns must be zero. The final commanded velocity and acceleration must be zero, permitting a position/yaw hold beyond the last row. Acceleration is kinematic feedforward; gravity is handled by the controller. Internal command tensors contain 11 values: P(3), V(3), A(3), yaw, and zero yaw rate.

The general CSV format can contain varying yaw, but the current spline policy representation fixes yaw throughout an episode. The saved command file does not encode the initial cable shape/velocity or the model identity; replay requires a matching scene/model.

### Spline family

The generator represents position with a clamped, uniform-knot, degree-five B-spline:

```text
p_d(t) = sum_j B_j(t/T) C_j
v_d(t) = d p_d(t) / dt
a_d(t) = d² p_d(t) / dt²
```

The first three controls impose initial P/V/A. Three coincident final controls impose a learned endpoint with zero final V/A. The remaining controls shape the curve and are not interpolated waypoints. Simple interior knots give C4 continuity inside the maneuver; extension by a constant hold guarantees matching P/V/A, not necessarily matching higher derivatives at the join.

For `N` total controls, there are `N-6` free interior XYZ controls and one free XYZ endpoint: `3(N-6)+3` action values. The training default is **32 total controls -> 26 interior controls plus endpoint -> 81 values**. Initial P/V/A and yaw are not extra action outputs. Duration is currently fixed by configuration, not learned.

The separate manual [spline editor](../controllers/full_state_controller/bspline_window.py) still opens with two interior controls plus endpoint, corresponding to eight total controls. It supports adding/removing controls. Its small demonstration default has not been changed to the training default.

The GPU evaluator caches bases for a fixed count, duration, timestep, dtype, and device; it generates batches with analytic derivatives and supports autograd. The active physics uses float64. Changing duration requires a corresponding evaluator/sample grid; it must not silently change the controller timestep.

Derivative-control convex-hull bounds conservatively limit continuous-time speed and acceleration. Exceeding a bound rejects the command, without silently clipping or retiming it. Passing these bounds does not guarantee that the simulated or physical drone respects an operating envelope.

## 9. Episode environment and batched rollout

Sources: [tasks/whip/configuration.py](../tasks/whip/configuration.py), [tasks/whip/environment.py](../tasks/whip/environment.py), [batched_rollout.py](../simulation/batched_rollout.py).

### Configuration and observations

`TrainingConfiguration` defaults only the control count to 32. The caller must explicitly provide maneuver duration, post-maneuver hold duration, lower/upper XYZ offset bounds, maximum speed, and maximum acceleration. Durations must be whole 100-Hz intervals. There are no adopted task-limit values or target radius hidden in these settings.

`WhipEnvironment.observation()` returns structured initial drone tracking position/velocity, rotation/body angular velocity, full cable positions/velocities, and target position. It does not choose a neural-network architecture, flattening/normalization convention, state distribution, or target sampler.

The reusable learner described below accepts an explicit context tensor. It does not silently flatten this dictionary or use perfect simulated cable-node states as an approved deployable observation model. Choosing measured/estimated policy inputs remains a task-level decision.

### One action per episode

1. Receive a batch of normalized actions in `[-1, 1]`, ordered as all interior XYZ controls followed by endpoint XYZ.
2. Map each XYZ triple linearly into configured **world-axis offsets from the initial tracking position**. The same offset box applies to learned interior controls and endpoint. It is not rotated into a target-aligned frame.
3. Construct the spline with scene initial position/velocity, zero initial command acceleration, initial yaw held fixed, and zero final commanded velocity/acceleration.
4. Check finite/in-range actions and conservative derivative limits. Append the explicitly configured final hold.
5. Run all command intervals through the supplied coupled simulator, preserving its physical parameters, residual, and command delay.
6. Return commands, controls, derivative bounds, masks, and full simulated motion. The target is context only in this environment; it does not change decoding or dynamics, and the environment does not call the separate replay scorer.

For `K` intervals, command samples have `K+1` rows including the terminal row. The runner receives the first `K` rows; row `k` acts on `[k*dt, (k+1)*dt)`. Its state history contains `K+1` states. With zero post-hold duration, the rollout ends at the maneuver horizon and does not automatically flush the delay queue or simulate recovery to rest. A stopped command endpoint is not a guarantee of a stopped physical drone/cable.

### Runtime and returned data

Each call resets all lanes to their configured initial states. Delay queues initially contain position/yaw holds, matching the interactive runtime even for moving initial states. Batch size/initial states are fixed for a runner instance; targets and actions can vary between calls. Construct another runner to change that initial batch.

GPU graph capture reuses the same forward transition for a fixed batch. There are no device-to-host transfers in the time loop. The runner is forward-only (`no_grad`) and is not safe for concurrent calls on one instance. Differentiable spline decoding and differentiable fitting must not be described as end-to-end policy differentiation through this episode API.

`BatchedRollout(..., pack_rejected=True)` enables execution-only packing on captured CUDA runners with more than one lane. Before integration it counts lanes accepted by the supplied admissibility mask and the whole-sequence finite/yaw-rate check. When accepted count is at most `floor(7*batch_size/8)`, only these lanes receive full repeated integration; nearly full batches use the unchanged full-width path. The one-eighth rejection threshold is an execution heuristic, not a task or learning threshold, and is not established as optimal across workloads. CPU, `graph=False`, batch-one and explicit `pack_rejected=False` retain the full path.

Rejected lanes remain frozen at their configured initial state. One full-width proposed hold step preserves their actual finite/rotation diagnostics, which are repeated because their state and applied command never change. Accepted results are scattered into the original order with all attempted samples, commands, rejection masks and failure penalties retained; there is no resampling or learner-batch change. A single auxiliary runner caches the accepted width, is rebuilt when that count changes, and refreshes initial states/holds when lane identities change. Returned histories own their data. The accepted-count read and packing can synchronize before the time loop; no claim of a wholly host-free rollout is made. See the bounded packing comparison below.

Returned histories include tracking position/velocity, body-to-world rotation, body angular velocity, every cable node's position/velocity, requested/applied commands, rest lengths, and marker indices. Derived interval acceleration uses tracking-velocity differences. Cable-length errors are signed edge-length deviations; no task-specific acceptance threshold is imposed.

`EpisodeResult.trajectory(index)` hands an accepted command to the existing `FullStateTrajectory` interface for CSV export. The Training window lists saved checkpoint/evaluation CSV paths; its Copy CSV path action allows selection in the existing full-state controller UI. The training window does not run the simulator viewer or replay automatically.

### Validity is not success

- `action_valid`: finite normalized action components within `[-1, 1]`.
- `context_valid`: finite target position.
- Environment `command_valid`: valid action, finite generated commands, and configured derivative bounds satisfied.
- Rollout `command_valid`: per-interval finite command with zero yaw-rate feedforward.
- `step_finite` / `step_rotation_valid`: diagnostics on proposed integration output. Rotation checks require near-orthogonality and determinant near +1 at the implemented 1e-6 thresholds.
- `state_valid`: cumulative eligibility/numerical validity mask. Rejected command sequences invalidate the whole lane. A lane returning an invalid numerical state freezes at its last valid state and stays masked while other lanes continue.
- `valid`: combined input/command/numerical validity, **not a physical safety certificate, a whip detector, or target success**.

Frozen/masked states are placeholders and must never earn reward. A solver exception can still abort the entire call; masking handles returned invalid states, not every possible exception. No collision, flight-envelope, or target-contact detector is implemented in this environment.

The optional [workspace wrappers](../tasks/whip/workspace.py) add the explicitly selected cylindrical task constraint without changing this base environment: outside-cylinder spline controls reject the command, and outside-cylinder sampled drone motion rejects the return. Presets without `[workspace]` continue to use the original environment and reward classes. Details and limits are below.

### Reusable direct episodic learner

Sources: [configuration.py](../training/configuration.py), [networks.py](../training/networks.py), [trust_region.py](../training/trust_region.py), [episodic.py](../training/episodic.py).

The selected direction is **direct episodic learning**. No optimization teacher, MBPO, PPO clipping, transformer critic, experience replay, curriculum, or end-to-end physics gradient was added. This implementation follows the episodic importance-ratio objective and diagonal KL-projection construction in [Otto et al., Deep Black-Box Reinforcement Learning with Movement Primitives, Section 3 and Appendix A](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v205/otto23a/otto23a.pdf). It is an independent implementation with explicit engineering adaptations, not a reproduction of the paper's complete benchmark implementation or results.

**Policy and context.** `EpisodicLearner` takes `LearnerConfiguration(context_size, action_size)`, a CPU/CUDA device, optional context center/scale, optional initial normalized action, optional fixed square full-rank `action_matrix`, and JSON-compatible provenance metadata. Context order, meaning, units, initial-state distribution and target distribution belong to the caller. Normalization `(context-center)/scale` is frozen, stored in checkpoints and defaults to identity; it is not fitted from evaluation data. The policy and separate value network each default to two 64-unit Tanh hidden layers. Policy mean output weights start at zero. Without a matrix its bias initializes the requested action (zero by default); with a matrix its bias starts at zero and a fixed action offset initializes the requested action. None of these choices certifies initial spline feasibility.

The policy samples `z ~ Normal(mu(c), diag(exp(2*log_std)))`, with one learned, context-independent log-standard-deviation vector. The legacy/default generic map supplies `a=tanh(z)`. The optional map supplies `a=tanh(b+M z)`, where `M` is the caller's fixed full-rank matrix and `b=atanh(initial_action)` is fixed. In the current 32-control whip environment, `action_size=81`; the learner itself is dimension-independent. Deterministic inference transforms `mu(c)` through the same map; it is not the mathematical expectation of the squashed distribution. All 81 action freedoms remain. No clipping, rejection sampling or noise at individual 100-Hz timesteps is performed. Squashing bounds individual action components only; the environment retains all existing derivative and numerical-validity checks.

Networks default to float32; float64 is configurable. Latent sampling, joint log densities, importance ratios, KL measurements, projection calculations and the optional matrix/offset use float64 on the selected device. The retained latent avoids inverse-tanh instability at saturated actions. A common fixed invertible affine-plus-tanh transform preserves KL and its Jacobian cancels in the joint importance ratio, so these calculations remain in diagonal latent coordinates. Finite-precision saturation is not an exactly invertible numerical map; the implementation retains the generating latent rather than reconstructing it from rounded actions. Densities are summed across all action dimensions, not averaged or clipped. There is no entropy bonus, GAE or time-discounting layer hidden in the learner. Any time-dependent task score must be supplied explicitly by the task.

**Update.** A batch contains one context, sampled latent/action, behavior mean/log-scale/log-density and pre-update value estimate per episode. Given one finite return `R_i` for every sampled row, the value baseline minimizes mean squared error to `R_i`. The actor uses the fixed pre-update advantage `A_i=R_i-V_old(c_i)`, scaled by the batch RMS of these advantages (floor 1e-8, no mean subtraction). It maximizes the importance-ratio surrogate under the projected policy, with a KL penalty pulling the raw network toward the detached projection. This scaling is an implementation choice, not a claim of an exactly unbiased finite-batch gradient estimator.

For proposed diagonal Gaussian `(mu,sigma)` and fixed behavior `(mu0,sigma0)`, the two limits apply to the actual contributions to `KL(new || old)`:

```text
K_mean = 0.5 sum_j ((mu_j-mu0_j)/sigma0_j)^2
K_cov  = 0.5 sum_j (r_j - 1 - log(r_j)),  r_j=sigma_j^2/sigma0_j^2
```

The mean is projected radially in the old covariance metric. Covariance minimizes `KL(projected || proposed)` subject to its covariance KL bound relative to the behavior policy. Writing `t_j=projected_variance_j/old_variance_j`, its solution is `t_j=(1+eta)/(1/r_j+eta)`. The positive multiplier is solved with 64 batched bisection steps in log space; an implicit first-order derivative accounts for the active constraint. Old distributions are detached. No NumPy/C++ optimizer or device-to-host transfer is used inside this projection solver. This is a numerical implementation of the projection, not infinite-precision arithmetic; second derivatives through the custom covariance projection are unsupported.

After the tentative actor update, **parameter backtracking** checks the actual unprojected inference network against both KL limits at every sampled context, and checks that its sampled surrogate has not decreased. It tries fractions `1, 1/2, ...` of the parameter displacement. If no candidate is acceptable, it restores the old actor. A shortened/rejected actor update restores the actor optimizer's pre-update state. This guard is an implementation addition; its checks do not guarantee KL bounds on unseen contexts, physical safety or improved true expected reward. Numerical acceptance allowances are 1e-7 for float32 networks and 1e-12 for float64 networks. A failed numerical update restores both networks and both optimizer states.

Default learner settings are actor/value Adam rates 3e-4/1e-3, 10 full-batch epochs per update, mean/covariance KL limits .01/.01, projection penalty coefficient 1, gradient norm limit 1, latent initial standard deviation .2, log-standard-deviation limits [-5,1], and 16 backtracking attempts. They are editable learner defaults, **not tuned AeroWhip training settings, whip thresholds or approved physical limits**. The optional map induces a full correlated pre-tanh covariance `M diag(sigma²) Mᵀ`, but only latent diagonal variances are learned in this fixed basis. An unrestricted learned full-covariance parameterization and adaptive entropy schedules are not implemented.

**Caller interface.** `sample(contexts)` opens one on-policy batch. The caller evaluates `batch.actions` and calls `update(batch, returns)` with one explicit finite score per row. For the whip environment, actions are already float64 on the learner device and can be passed to `environment.run(actions, targets)` when devices agree. Task scoring must honor validity masks and define failure handling; the learner adds no hidden failure score and does not drop failed rows. The complete batch must be updated or discarded before another is sampled; consumed/stale batches, edited action/behavior-density inconsistencies and nonfinite returns are rejected. The batch retains snapshots of the optional matrix/offset; changing either before update is rejected. These are fixed buffers, not optimizer parameters. `discard_batch()` abandons a whole batch after, for example, an evaluator exception. `act(contexts)` is deterministic inference and does not consume sampling RNG.

**Checkpointing.** `save(path)` writes a new file only, refusing overwrite and outstanding batches. It retains configuration, frozen normalization, network and Adam states, update count, learner sampling RNG and caller-provided metadata. Identity-map saves retain schema `episodic_diagonal_trpl_v1`; correlated-map saves use `episodic_affine_tanh_trpl_v2` and retain the exact float64 matrix and offset in the policy state. Both schemas load without migrating or overwriting old files. A new correlated initialization is not a continuation of an old independent policy. Checkpoints do not save the simulator, context sampler or an external experiment's RNG. `load(path, device=..., expected_metadata=...)` uses weights-only loading and can reject mismatched provenance. CPU/CUDA sampling streams are not interchangeable: changing device type requires explicit `reset_sampling_rng=True`. Exact continuation was checked on the same CPU or CUDA device, not across GPU models/library versions. Metadata is caller-supplied; the generic learner does not automatically detect physical-model or reward updates. Neither checkpoint saves nor loads promote a physics model.

**Learner verification evidence.** In-process checks covered projection identity/active constraints, finite-difference gradients on CPU/CUDA, an independent scalar covariance-bound reference, 81-dimensional KL bounds, RNG isolation, stale/nonfinite batch handling, exclusive checkpoint save and exact same-device resumed updates, aggressive-update backtracking, and injected numerical-failure rollback. A 40-update synthetic 81-action contextual task reduced deterministic error on its fixed evaluation contexts. A separate four-lane, 0.20 s active-model CUDA check passed 81-parameter samples through the unchanged spline/environment, received finite synthetic returns, updated the learner, and constructed a 21-row controller-compatible trajectory. Its tiny offset box and synthetic score were temporary check settings, not adopted task settings. These checks are not whip training, a convergence study, physical validation, or a retained paper-ready experiment. The subsequently implemented single-scenario return/launcher is described below; its initialization run made zero policy updates. Later training retained updated weights, but no useful learned whip or deployed initial-state encoder is established.

### Smooth spline exploration

Sources: [exploration.py](../trajectories/exploration.py), [networks.py](../training/networks.py), [experiment.py](../tasks/whip/experiment.py). This is an explicit exploration prior, not a new trajectory family, whip definition or reward. The spline decoder still receives 81 normalized action values and uses all 32 quintic controls.

For one scalar axis, `E` embeds 27 free-control perturbations into 32 controls: the first three perturbations are zero to preserve initial P/V/A; the last free endpoint perturbation is repeated three times to preserve zero final V/A. Applying the existing analytic acceleration basis to `E` gives the sampled acceleration design matrix `A`. On the native 100-Hz command grid, trapezoidal endpoint weights give:

```text
Q = (dt/T) Aᵀ W A
R = I + tau⁴ Q
L0 = symmetric_inverse_square_root(R)
L = amplitude_scale * L0 / sqrt(mean(row_squared_norm(L0)))
M = L ⊗ I3
```

`Q` measures time-average squared acceleration of scalar normalized free-control perturbations before the nonlinear action map and physical offset scaling. `tau` is a regularization time in seconds, not an exact correlation length. No eigenmodes are truncated; positive definiteness and numerical full rank are checked. The average squared row norm of `L` is `amplitude_scale²`, controlling average initial pre-tanh variance, not a physical displacement guarantee. The fixed smoothness prior does not guarantee smooth bounded acceleration after tanh for arbitrary learned means/variances. The unchanged derivative-control checks remain authoritative.

The task builds this small CPU float64 eigendecomposition once per learner/seed. Sampling and inference apply the fixed matrix in one batched float64 tensor multiplication on the learner device; nothing is added to each 100-Hz physics step. The reusable learner has no spline/physics imports. No simulation-throughput improvement is claimed for this change.

**Command-only calibration (2026-09-21).** Retained [calibration script and results](../../AeroWhip_archive/20260922-cleanup/runs/exploration/20260921-200058/calibration.json) compare independent exploration with 15 combinations of `tau` in {0.5,1,2} s and amplitude scale in {1,4,8,12,16}. Each variant uses the same three seeded Gaussian streams, 256 commands per seed, 768 commands total. The independent seed-0 commands reproduce the first sampled batch of the stopped 54-update run. No physics, reward scoring or optimizer step is used for selection. All checked commands have finite P/V/A and the unchanged endpoint constraints.

The selected initialization setting is **tau=2 s, amplitude_scale=12**. Median maximum command excursion from the initial tracking point changes from 0.0272792 m to 0.3687854 m; command-bound acceptance changes from 94.140625% to 100% on these 768 samples. The selected setting has 90th-percentile speed/acceleration bounds 0.844995 m/s and 9.682522 m/s², compared with unchanged limits 4 m/s and 15 m/s². Its matrix has rank 81 and condition number 109.5165. Scale 16 at the same time parameter gave larger median excursion (0.486126 m) but lower acceptance (97.65625%); scale 12 was selected for more bound margin, not as an optimal exploration distribution. ### Reach-first experiment (implemented, not trained)

Sources: [reach.py](../tasks/whip/reach.py), [reach preset](../../AeroWhip_archive/20260922-cleanup/configs/experiments/whip_reach_training.toml), [comparison runner](../tasks/whip/analysis/run_reach_comparison.py). The receiving region is a **finite vertical rectangle**, centered on the configured target. Its horizontal normal points from initial drone XY toward target XY. The preset uses center (1.10,0,1.75) m, full width **0.80 m**, full height **0.60 m**: x=1.10, y in [-0.40,0.40], z in [1.45,2.05]. It is wholly outside the 0.50 m drone cylinder. This is neither a target sphere nor a half-space, and passage through the plane is not an interpolated collision/contact event.

For tip displacement from window center, resolve horizontal forward coordinate `f`, horizontal sideways coordinate `s`, and vertical coordinate `h`. At each state sample:

```text
d(t) = sqrt(f(t)^2 + max(|s(t)|-width/2,0)^2 + max(|h(t)|-height/2,0)^2)
P(t) = clamp(1 - d(t)/d(0), 0, 1)
Q(t) = existing peak-associated bending B(t) * world-tip speed score V(t)
S(t) = P(t) * (1 + 0.10 * Q(t))
return = max_t S(t) - existing command-acceleration cost
```

The combined-score maximum can select a different time from the closest approach. No extra terminal state, recovery requirement, speed threshold or binary success flag is introduced. Failure overrides remain -1.

Legacy global peak tuples are not relabeled as reach events. `closest_distance_mean_m` remains the historical point-center diagnostic for compatibility; explicitly named window-distance fields drive reach UI labels.

### Implemented measurement layer

Source: [whip_metrics.py](../tasks/whip/metrics.py). `WhipMetrics` is independent of the physics, controller and environment execution. Construct it from the rollout's rest lengths, time grid and **four explicitly supplied material-coordinate boundaries**, then pass cable positions, velocities and `state_valid`. The boundaries must span `[0, L]` and define proximal, middle and distal regions. This measurement API has no default region sizes or smoothing scale. The experiment now explicitly selects equal material thirds; that choice is not implicit in the reusable measurement layer.

```python
metrics = WhipMetrics(rollout.cable_rest_lengths, rollout.time_s,
                      region_boundaries_m=boundaries)
measured = metrics(rollout.cable_positions, rollout.cable_velocities,
                   rollout.state_valid)
```

The mesh/time grid, dtype, device and boundaries are fixed per evaluator; batch size can vary. Reuse the evaluator for matching subsequent batches. It is not automatically called by `WhipEnvironment.run`, and adds no viewer/UI. Input positions and velocities are not modified or realigned. Its time grid need not be uniform, although current simulator output remains 100 Hz.

**Bending:** for rest edge lengths `l_i`, material node coordinate `s_i = sum_{j<i} l_j`, and unit current edge tangents `t_i`, interior node `i` has:

```text
d_i = (l_(i-1) + l_i) / 2
theta_i = atan2(||t_(i-1) x t_i||, t_(i-1) . t_i)
k_i = theta_i / d_i
w_i = k_i² d_i
```

`theta_i` is an unsigned turning angle in radians; `k_i` is a material-length turning-density estimate in 1/m. This diagnostic is **not** the solver's `2*tan(theta/2)` curvature-binormal formula, and `sum(w_i)` is not claimed to be the model's bending energy. The angle remains finite at a reversal; that does not certify such a configuration as physically/numerically admissible for DDER. Zero-length edges invalidate the sample. Stretch is not corrected here; inspect rollout length errors separately.

Returned bend histories include the full angle/curvature distribution, total turning angle, squared-curvature integral `sum(w_i)`, and its weighted material centroid/spread:

```text
centroid = sum_i(w_i s_i) / sum_i(w_i)
spread² = sum_i(w_i (s_i - centroid)²) / sum_i(w_i)
```

The centroid's finite-difference rate is reported at interval midpoints, only between two valid, defined adjacent samples. Positive rate is motion of this statistic toward the tip, **not an estimated wave speed**. Two stationary bends changing relative strength can move the centroid without a travelling bend; inspecting the full curvature history is essential. With exactly zero squared curvature, centroid/spread/rate are undefined (NaN). No significance threshold suppresses near-straight roundoff or weak vibrations; a finite centroid alone is not evidence of substantial bending.

For regional RMS curvature, each interior node's `k_i` is treated as constant on its dual cell between neighboring rest-edge midpoints. Integrate `k_i²` using the exact region/dual-cell overlap and divide by supported length before taking the square root. There is no curvature estimate at the two endpoint half-edges: `region_bend_support_m` reports the actual covered length within each region, excluding those half-edges; a region with no interior support has NaN curvature, not a fabricated zero. These material weights avoid node-count weighting, but do not prove mesh independence for sharp folds.

**Motion and timing:** return regional RMS speeds and tip speed, both in the world frame and after subtracting attachment velocity. Regional speeds integrate squared, piecewise-linear interpolated node velocity over rest arclength, divided by the full region length. This is length weighting, not mass weighting or kinetic energy. Relative speeds remove uniform translation, **not** rigid rotation. All histories retain their sample timestamps; no temporal smoothing, global-peak matching, propagation delay fitting or sub-sample timing is imposed. There is no coherent-event detector yet, and no target-distance calculation in this layer.

**Validity and computation:** outputs are batch-major. Input `state_valid`, finite positions/velocities, nonzero edge lengths and finite derived values determine `sample_valid`. Invalid samples are NaN throughout measurement histories and cannot contribute a centroid difference across a gap. A valid straight cable can still have an undefined bend centroid. Sample validity is not physical safety or whip success. Construction validates values with possible GPU synchronization; evaluation is forward-only, tensor-vectorized over batches/time/nodes/regions, with no host value checks or CPU transfers. CUDA graph capture was checked; no gradients through this diagnostic API are promised.

### Isolated Stage-1 objective: approved experimental shaping

Sources: [stage1.py](../tasks/whip/stage1.py), [stage1_configuration.py](../tasks/whip/stage1_configuration.py), and [whip_stage1.toml](../../AeroWhip_archive/20260922-cleanup/configs/experiments/whip_stage1.toml). The scorer itself does not instantiate or change the simulator; the training adapter below reuses the existing frozen dynamics.

For every candidate bend window `W=[a,b]` and associated release sample `t`, the implemented objective is:

```text
sat(x,k) = max(x,0) / (max(x,0) + k)
B = rho² * sat(minimum_turn, 0.5 rad) * sat(travel, 0.2 L)
    * exp(-(maximum_spread / (0.25 L))^4)
P = sat(nonrigid_tip_speed, 0.5 m/s) * sat(relative_speed_rise, 0.5 m/s)
F = sat(tip_velocity . requested_direction, 1 m/s)
M = (B P F)^(1/3)
A = exp(-0.5 * [(height_error / 0.35 m)^2
               + (speed_matching_error / 1.5 m/s)^2 + (direction_error / 45 degrees)^2])
valid episode: R = max_(W,t) [M(W,t) A(t) (1 - 0.05 t / T)] - C
invalid episode: R = -1
```

`matching.speed_mode` defaults to `match`: `speed_matching_error = v_tip - v_request`. The separate [minimum-speed preset](../configs/experiments/whip_stage1_minimum_speed.toml) explicitly selects `minimum`: `speed_matching_error = min(v_tip - v_request, 0)`. Thus its speed factor is `exp(-0.5*(max(0,3-v_tip)/1.5)^2)`: motion above 3 m/s no longer loses speed-matching credit. This is a soft shortfall penalty, not a hard success threshold or unbounded speed bonus. Signed speed-error diagnostics remain measured minus requested in both modes. All terms still select the same event jointly; no separate favorable speed peak is substituted.

`T=3.5 s` is the full configured episode horizon. The Gaussian and time factor are **outside** the inherited cubic root. The world tip-speed magnitude is used for request matching; `F` separately rewards positive projection onto the requested direction. Direction error is the unsigned 3-D angle to world +X, not separate yaw/elevation errors. Zero measured tip speed gets zero matching credit. Gaussian widths are shaping scales, not hit tolerances or success thresholds. Timing only changes an otherwise identical event's credit by at most 5%; it neither enforces a first qualifying event nor prohibits repeated swings.

`M` preserves the overnight research proxy mathematics: material-coordinate Gaussian smoothing of unsigned turning density with sigma `0.05 L`; turn-weighted centroid/spread; positive advection-regression coherence `rho`; travel equal to the smaller of positive centroid shift and fitted transport speed times window duration; and a material-weighted least-squares removal of instantaneous rigid translation/rotation for the tip-speed residual. Relative-speed rise subtracts the mean attachment-relative tip speed over the first 20% of the bend window. Numerical denominator floors and pseudoinverse relative tolerance `1e-8` retain the research implementation. This smoothed, turn-weighted calculation differs from the unsmoothed curvature-squared descriptive centroids in `SkillMetrics`; neither is physical bending energy or an identified travelling packet. The rigid-motion residual is not the fitted dynamics residual network.

The cached search uses window durations 0.2/0.4/0.6/0.8 s, starts every 0.05 s beginning at elapsed 0.03 s, bend ends no later than 3.03 s, and release delays 0-0.15 s at 100 Hz. This produces **3,264 candidate triples** in the configured horizon. All terms are combined before selecting the best release/window; the selector does not first choose the old mechanics winner. The direction previously derived from a target is now supplied by the request. The original height-only Stage 1 has no target position or target-distance reward; the new optional directed version below adds finite-wall distance shaping. Configuration event bounds explicitly match the current three-step command delay; future changes require a corresponding reviewed configuration.

`Stage1Scorer(rest_lengths, time_s, configuration)` returns scores, whole-history numerical validity, a positive-score-event mask, selected `[start,end,release]` indices, and raw component/error measurements. It accepts `(B,T,N,3)` positions/velocities plus `(B,T)` validity. Invalid rows are isolated before linear algebra and have NaN scores; a valid zero-score row has score zero, indices `-1` and NaN selected-event components. `has_event` means positive proxy credit only, not whipping. The time origin may be nonzero, but the supplied grid must span the configured horizon at 100 Hz.

`Stage1Reward(rest_lengths, time_s, configuration)` wraps the scorer for a `BatchedRolloutResult`, aligned `(B,T,11)` P/V/A/yaw/yaw-rate commands, and an explicit caller-supplied command/action admissibility mask. Existing decoding must still enforce continuous command bounds. The wrapper checks the full sampled episode's numerical masks, mesh/time identity, drone box/cylinder, tilt, cable minimum height and finite commands. A later failure overrides an earlier event. `C` retains the existing `0.01 * mean(||command_acceleration / 15 m/s²||²)` over the 300 left-endpoint maneuver samples, excluding the hold; no state truncation or new effort definition was introduced.

### Local bend tracking diagnostic

Sources: [bend_tracking.py](../tasks/whip/analysis/bend_tracking.py), [synthetic_cases.py](../tasks/whip/analysis/synthetic_cases.py), [run_bend_analysis.py](../tasks/whip/analysis/run_bend_analysis.py). Run the last file directly in PyCharm without arguments. It reads existing data and writes a new timestamped `runs/bend_analysis/` directory. It does not run dynamics, fitting, policy training, a GUI, or a reward calculation. The reusable simulator and existing training definitions remain unchanged.

`measure_bends(time, positions, material_s, tip_lag_s=0.15)` accepts one `(T,N,3)` position history and fixed rest/material coordinates. It computes the same unsigned turning-angle/rest-dual-length diagnostic described above, not the DDER solver's curvature or bending energy. It preserves invalid rows, uses no smoothing or physical amplitude threshold, and does not interpolate missing observations. A numerical-only roundoff allowance accounts for curvature arithmetic and subtraction of world-coordinate positions. This is not a measurement-noise model.

For each interior local curvature maximum (including flat maxima), adjacent troughs define a local baseline equal to the higher trough. The connected piecewise-linear lobe above that baseline gives:

```text
bend strength A = integral_lobe max(kappa(s) - baseline, 0) ds  [rad]
width = right baseline crossing - left baseline crossing      [m]
```

This is local excess turning angle, not peak curvature, total whole-cable turning, or a conserved energy packet. Peak position is a three-point quadratic estimate where valid, otherwise a plateau midpoint. The original samples determine lobe integration; peak interpolation adds no measured spatial resolution. Endpoint maxima and lobes without an observed interior trough on both sides are retained as boundary-censored features but excluded from interior tracking. A flat trough plateau extending into the observed interior is supported even if it also reaches a boundary.

Only consecutive valid rows can be linked. The original sampled-maximum/plateau interval must intersect the other frame's lobe in both directions, and the correspondence must be unique at both ends. Quadratic peak interpolation is descriptive only: it cannot decide branch identity. Competing matches terminate the involved tracks and start flagged fragments. Explicit invalid rows, disappearance and unresolved correspondence are not bridged. The nonuniform-time API does not infer missing samples from a finite timestamp jump; callers must retain missing observations as NaN rows. The recorded-data runner separately verifies native 100-Hz timing. Every fragment, including singletons, is retained. These conservative geometric links do not establish causal wave identity, and no resolved link does **not** establish non-whipping.

Each track reports integrated strength/width histories, signed net material travel and mean propagation rate (net travel divided by elapsed time). Tip velocity is derived identically for controlled, recorded and saved simulation positions using nonuniform central differences inside contiguous valid runs, with one-sided endpoint differences; isolated valid samples remain undefined. Tip speed at track end and the largest speed in the following 0.15 s of available contiguous data are reported separately. The actual observed lag duration is recorded. This descriptive window is not a whip threshold, and association does not prove that bending caused tip acceleration. No scalar combination or binary classification is adopted.

### Working physical description

The proposed operational description is a substantial, localized deformation travelling toward the free end, accompanied by a temporally related distal motion pulse. Candidate evidence includes coherent bend progression in material coordinates, ordered proximal-to-distal motion, and a meaningful distal release. The pilot below selects provisional regions, event timing and continuous amplitude scales; it does not track a localized travelling bend or finalize physical acceptance criteria.

A fast tip alone is insufficient: a cable moving approximately as a fixed shape under translation and rotation can have a fast tip without a travelling bending pattern. Target contact alone is likewise not a whip definition. Velocity progression is a kinematic proxy, not a direct measurement of energy flux.

The old repository's `planning/whip_objective.py` compares against a saved preferred folding demonstration; its own description says this is not a validated wave detector. `planning/free_whip.py` contains optional ordered velocity-peak and pullback proxies. Those old scores, numerical thresholds, and reversal preferences have **not** been adopted as the new definition.

### Research grounding and limits

- McMillen and Goriely, [Whip waves](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-2789(03)00221-5), Physica D, 2003: travelling-wave mechanics in an elastic rod, including tapering and boundary-condition effects. Its model is not identical to this untapered marker-loaded aerial cable.
- Krotov et al., [Motor control beyond reach—how humans hit a target with a whip](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9533004/), Royal Society Open Science, 2022: observed cascade-like unfolding in human whip targeting. These observations motivate diagnostics; they do not supply a validated classifier for our hardware.

Neither citation by itself justifies declaring our proposed thresholds universal or proving energy transfer from a speed plot. The manuscript must distinguish the adopted operational definition from the mechanisms that motivate it.

### Provisional replay-scoring pilot

Sources: [scoring.py](../tasks/whip/scoring.py), [run_whip_scoring.py](../tasks/whip/analysis/run_whip_scoring.py). Run the latter directly in PyCharm; no arguments, simulation rerun, viewer modifications or policy training. `SOURCE_RUN` identifies the retained histories explicitly. Original artifacts are hash-verified and not overwritten. New timestamped results contain configuration, input/source hashes, source snapshots, component summaries, score histories and annotated diagnostic plots.

`WhipScoreConfiguration` defaults: equal material thirds, baseline window 0.30 s, adjacent-region delay 0.02–0.25 s, maximum proximal-to-distal duration 0.40 s, tip delay 0–0.15 s, dimensionless bend reference 1, speed reference 5 m/s, Gaussian target width 0.30 m, whip/target weights 0.2/0.8. These are approved **pilot settings**, not measured physical thresholds or tuned training settings. Target width is not a contact radius.

For each region j, use dimensionless bending `b_j(t)=L*regional_rms_curvature_j(t)` and recent increase `p_j(t)=max(0,b_j(t)-min_{u in [t-0.30,t]} b_j(u))`. Baselines use available preceding samples, including before the scoring window; at the history start they are truncated rather than zero-padded. Let `a_j=p_j/(p_j+1)`. For ordered sample times P/M/D satisfying the timing limits, `B=(a_P*a_M*a_D)^(1/3)`. At the associated tip sample t, `V=world_tip_speed/(world_tip_speed+5)`, `A=exp(-0.5*(distance_to_archived_target/0.30)^2)`. The pilot score is the maximum of `B*V*(0.2+0.8*A)` over admissible tuples. Speed and distance are evaluated at the **same 100-Hz sample**. No sub-sample interpolation or collision/impact model is claimed; fast between-sample passages can be missed. Without a supplied target, score is unweighted `B*V`, with target fields unavailable; this is used for Gentle and for a separate quality-only comparison of all cases.

The scorer takes batched dimensionless regional bending, world tip position/velocity, validity, explicit per-lane regional-event windows, and optional targets. Callers must construct bending with the configured material regions; the analysis runner checks archived region boundaries. All P/M/D samples must lie inside the supplied closed window. Tip sampling can extend 0.15 s past its end. The uniform grid and all timing parameters must agree in integer sample steps. Rolling maxima over bounded lag windows perform the exact discrete tuple search without a cubic-in-history candidate tensor. Evaluation stays on the input device, is forward-only, and supports varying batch size; construction may synchronize. The peak variant restricts P/M/D to positive **local maxima of the existing bend-increase signal p_j**, not maxima of raw curvature. A candidate must strictly exceed the preceding sample and strictly exceed the sample following its full equal-valued plateau. The mask is computed across the full supplied history, then candidate times are restricted to the same maneuver window.
